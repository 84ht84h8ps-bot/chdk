Masterclass: HDR Panorama on the EOS M3

Complete step-by-step tutorial: shooting the exposure matrix with pano_helper and 1-click merging in Lightroom

The Concept: The Exposure Matrix

An HDR Panorama combines multi-exposure bracketing with panoramic stitching. When shooting into high-contrast sunsets, single-exposure panoramas blow out the sun or plunge foregrounds into black noise.

By shooting a 3-stop bracket (−2 EV, 0 EV, +2 EV) at every single rotation angle, you capture the entire 160° landscape with zero blown highlights and zero noisy shadows:

6 Panels × 3 Bracketed Exposures = 18 RAW Frames Total

Step 1: Shooting the HDR Matrix with pano_helper.lua

  1. Mount your camera vertically on your tripod. Set mode dial to M (Manual) at f/8, ISO 100.
  2. Set White Balance to Daylight (5200K) in the Canon menu.
  3. Press M-Fn (<ALT>) → MENU → Script → Load Script from File... → select pano_helper.lua.
  4. Configure these exact parameters:
    • Number of Pano Panels: 6
    • Pause to Rotate Tripod (sec): 5 (gives a comfortable window to rotate smoothly)
    • Bracket Each Panel?: Set to 1 ← CRUCIAL: Enables HDR bracketing at each angle!
  5. Press MENU to exit. Aim at your starting frame (far left) and press the Shutter button.
  6. The Shooting Cycle:
    • The camera locks settings and fires 3 rapid bracketed shots: −2 EV (sky), 0 EV (midtones), and +2 EV (shadows).
    • You hear a BEEP!
    • Smoothly rotate your tripod head to the right by ~30% overlap during the 5-second pause.
    • Take your hands off. The camera automatically fires the next 3 bracketed shots!
    • Repeat across all 6 panels until the script finishes.

Step 2: Lightroom's 1-Click “HDR Panorama” Merge

Do not waste time manually merging each 3-shot bracket first. Lightroom has a dedicated engine that merges brackets and stitches the panorama simultaneously:

1

Select All 18 RAWs
In Lightroom Library grid (G), select all 18 files from the session at once.

→
2

Cmd + Shift + M
Right-click → Photo Merge → HDR Panorama (Shortcut: Cmd + Shift + M on Mac / Ctrl + Shift + M on Win).

→
3

Lightroom AI Engine
Lightroom groups the 18 frames into 6 brackets, creates 32-bit HDRs in RAM, and stitches the vista in one pass!

→
4

Select Cylindrical
Choose Cylindrical projection, set Boundary Warp to 80-90, and check Fill Edges.

→
5

Merge 100MP DNG
Click Merge. Lightroom creates a single colossal -HDRPano.dng file!

Step 3: Developing Your 32-Bit HDR Panorama

Open the new -HDRPano.dng file in Lightroom's Develop Module (D):

  • Highlights (−60 to −90): The blazing sun and sunset clouds reveal incredible orange and purple textures without gray muddy clipping.
  • Shadows (+30 to +50): Dark foreground trees and rocks emerge with crystal clarity and zero chrominance noise because they were exposed at +2 EV in the bracket!
  • Dehaze (+10) & Clarity (+15): Cuts atmospheric haze across distant mountains while maintaining natural foreground colors.
  • Zoom to 100%: Pan across your 100+ megapixel image to inspect tack-sharp leaves, branches, and rock textures miles in the distance.
