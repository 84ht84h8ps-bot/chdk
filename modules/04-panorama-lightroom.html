Panoramic Landscape & Lightroom Stitching

Step-by-step field execution with pano_helper.lua and seamless stitching in Lightroom

Step 1: Physical Tripod & Camera Alignment

📐

Mount Vertically (Portrait)

Mount your camera in portrait orientation using an L-bracket or tilted tripod head. Vertical frames give you 6000 vertical pixels of resolution and natural landscape proportions.

⚖️

Level Your Tripod Base

Ensure your tripod head rotates on a perfectly horizontal plane. If the tripod is tilted, your panoramic sweep will curve like a banana, forcing severe cropping.

🎯

Lock Camera Settings

Dial to M (Manual) at f/8, ISO 100. White Balance set to Daylight (5200K)—never Auto White Balance!

Step 2: Shooting with pano_helper.lua

This script completely prevents exposure and focus shifts between panels and paces your tripod rotations with audio beeps:

  1. Aim the camera at the primary midtone region of your vista.
  2. Press M-Fn (<ALT>) → MENU → Script → Load Script from File... → select pano_helper.lua.
  3. Set Number of Pano Panels to 6 (or your chosen count covering your sweep with 30% overlap).
  4. Set Pause to Rotate Tripod (sec) to 4.
  5. Set Bracket Each Panel? to 0 (standard single exposure).
  6. Press MENU to close. Frame your first panel (far left) and press the Shutter button.
  7. Listen for the Beep:
    • The camera meters and locks exposure and focus, then shoots Panel 1.
    • You will hear an audio BEEP!
    • Smoothly turn your tripod head to the right by ~30% overlap during the 4-second pause.
    • Take your hands off the camera. Panel 2 fires automatically.
    • Repeat until the script double-beeps to signal the panorama is complete!

Panorama Field Gotchas & Troubleshooting

Problem Cause Solution
Lightroom says “Unable to merge” Overlap between panels was too small (<25%) Turn tripod less between shots; ensure 30% to 40% overlap between adjacent frames.
Sky has vertical stripes White Balance or Shutter changed Always use pano_helper.lua and lock WB to Daylight in Canon menu.
Foreground objects don't align Parallax error (camera pivoted behind lens nodal point) Keep prominent foreground rocks at least 2 meters away when panning on a standard tripod head.
